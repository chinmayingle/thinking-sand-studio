# Upload text: "Cache beats cores" (Short, 55.8 s)

**Title:** 2 More Cores: +3% FPS. 6x the Cache: +45%
**Alternates:**
- Why More CPU Cores Barely Help Your Games
- 6 Cores vs 8 Cores Tied. Cache Won by 55%

**Description:**
Going from 6 to 8 CPU cores made games about 3% faster on average. Going from 16 MB to 96 MB of L3 cache made them 45% faster. Why?

Cache is a small shelf of data right next to your CPU cores. RAM is a warehouse far away. When the data a core needs isn't on the shelf, the core stops and waits for the long trip to RAM. Games jump all over memory (enemies, physics, the world around you), so a bigger shelf means fewer long trips. More cores don't fix that; they just wait together. In Watch Dogs: Legion, 6 and 8 cores tied, while extra cache added 55%. That's the idea behind AMD's X3D chips with 3D V-Cache.

More cores or more cache: what's in your PC? 👇

Source: TechSpot, "CPU cache vs cores" (early 2024): Zen 3 Ryzen 5000 CPUs with 16 MB (5600G/5700G), 32 MB (5600X/5800X) and 96 MB (5600X3D/5800X3D) of L3, RTX 4090 at 1080p, DDR4-3600 CL14, 12-game average. Different CPU models, not cores switched off; clocks differ by up to 7%. Results at 1440p and 4K are usually closer because the GPU becomes the limit.
