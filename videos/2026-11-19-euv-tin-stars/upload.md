**Title:** Your GPU Was Printed With Light From 50,000 Tin Explosions a Second
**Alternates:**
- Chips Are Made by Blowing Up Molten Tin. 50,000 Times a Second.
- The Machine That Makes Tiny Suns to Print Your GPU

**Description:**
To print the chip in your GPU, a machine blows up 50,000 drops of molten tin every single second.

Each drop flies at 70 m/s. A weak laser pulse squashes it into a pancake, then a stronger pulse blasts it into plasma at about 500,000 K, hotter than the surface of the Sun. That plasma glows with extreme ultraviolet light, just 13.5 nm long, about 30× shorter than visible light. Air and glass absorb it, so it travels through a vacuum, bounced only by mirrors, until it draws the finest patterns on a silicon wafer, like the RTX 5090's chip with 92.2 billion transistors.

Did you know chips are printed with exploding tin? 👇

Sources: ASML, "The hottest thing about ASML machines" (droplets, pulses, temperature, 50,000 per second, 13.5 nm); ZEISS SMT, EUV lithography (air and glass absorb EUV, vacuum and mirrors only); TechPowerUp, RTX 5090 / GB202 (92.2 billion transistors, TSMC 5 nm-class EUV process).
Note: EUV prints a chip's finest layers; other layers use older deep-ultraviolet light. The droplet stream at the start is sped down to be visible.
